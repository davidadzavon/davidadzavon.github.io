abstract: >-
  Many studies find that drought fuels conflict by reducing agricultural
  income, yet how farmers' adaptation shapes these effects remains poorly
  understood. This paper tests whether fertilizer prices amplify
  drought-induced conflict in Africa over 1997–2019 by making adaptation
  more costly. Combining international prices and predetermined crop
  suitability in a transport-adjusted shift-share index, we estimate
  dynamic drought effects across 0.5° × 0.5° cells before and after
  prices surge in 2005–2008. Drought has little effect under low prices,
  but significantly larger effects afterward. Consistent with our
  mechanisms, we find that the price surge generates additional
  drought-induced conflicts equivalent to 25% of total conflicts
  observed afterward.
